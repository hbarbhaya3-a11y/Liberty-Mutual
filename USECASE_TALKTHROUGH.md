# Use‑Case Talk‑Throughs — hypothesis → configuration → results

Clear, professional narration grounded in the actual pages and levers. For each
use case: state the hypothesis, open the workbench and set a sensible policy,
run it, read the result. Numbers are what the screen shows.

---

## 1 · Deposit drift — "Targeted Deposit Defense"  *(consumer)*

**Hypothesis.** Across roughly **75,000** mass‑affluent savers — about **$2.1B** in
deposits — three signals are converging: balances are declining, outbound
transfers are accelerating, and everyday account activity is thinning. Together
they indicate this group is starting to shop for a better rate, typically about
**60 days** before the money actually moves. The hypothesis is that only a subset
is genuinely rate‑sensitive — roughly **30,000** — and we can retain them with a
modest, targeted rate rather than repricing the whole book. The pre‑simulation
estimate is **$15–25M** in retained deposits and a **1.8–2.8 point** drop in outflow.

**Configuration.** I open the workbench to set the policy. I scope it to the
rate‑sensitive eligible group, not the full cohort. I set the minimum qualifying
balance to **$25K** so we don't spend margin on small accounts. The offer is a
money‑market / CD at a **+40 basis‑point** ceiling — the most we'll pay. I deliver
it through app and email, with a banker for the largest balances, and hold back a
**20% control group** to prove the lift is real.

**Results.** The simulation returns **$19.3M** in retained deposits a year, with
outflow falling from **7.5% to 5.2%** — a **2.3‑point** improvement. The fairness
margin holds at **0.93**, above the 0.85 floor, so the policy is defensible. And it
suppresses offers to accounts predicted to stay regardless — no margin is given
away where it isn't needed.

## 2 · Idle cash — "Idle‑to‑Yield CD"

**Hypothesis.** We've identified **388,000** customers holding idle cash that earns
close to nothing, while competitors advertise **4%+**; some have begun searching
for and moving toward higher‑yield options. Of those, roughly **75,000** show
strong activation signals this cycle, and after excluding balances customers rely
on for everyday spending, about **20,000** are yield‑responsive and genuinely
surplus. The hypothesis: route that surplus into the best‑fit product — a **7‑month
CD**, high‑yield savings or money‑market — at a small rate uplift, capturing the
balance into yield before it leaves. Pre‑simulation estimate: **$3.5–5.2M** in
incremental relationship value.

**Configuration.** In the workbench I scope to the yield‑exposed eligible group. I
set the minimum idle balance to **$5K** so the offer only reaches meaningful
balances. The vehicle is the **7‑month CD**, and I set the rate uplift to
**+20 basis points** over base — attractive without giving away margin. I deliver
via app and email for mid‑value customers and a banker for high‑value ones, over
a **60‑day** dormancy window, with a **20% holdout**.

**Results.** Treating **67,500** in a 90/10 RCT, the result is **+$4.3M** in
incremental relationship value at a **5.4%** funded conversion (vs **1.9%**
organic) — **3,645** funded relationships and roughly **$265M** of balances
activated into yield, with idle outflow to competitors falling from **12% to 6.4%**
(**−5.6pp**). The suitability margin holds at **0.94** — and because we excluded
operating cash, no customer is locked out of money they need. The bank earns a
positive spread even after paying the higher rate.

## 3 · SMB growth — "Re‑bundle the off‑us flows"  *(small business)*

**Hypothesis.** **38,400** small businesses are showing expansion signals — new
locations, rising payroll, equipment purchases — and **61%** are already taking
their financing or card processing to a competitor: roughly **$2.1B** of financing
need. The hypothesis: win those flows back by leading with the single product each
business needs now, then attaching its everyday banking. Pre‑simulation estimate:
lift cross‑sell conversion into the **16–22%** range and add **$26–37M** in
first‑year revenue.

**Configuration.** I set the objective to conversion. I scope to the off‑us
financers — the businesses actively leaking. I pre‑approve a credit limit of
**$75K**, set intro pricing at **+75 basis points**, and choose **bundled**
packaging so the lead product carries the operating flows with it. I deliver
through a primary banker for larger relationships and in‑app pre‑approval for the
rest, with a **10% control group**. The credit‑risk limit and price floor are
enforced as hard constraints.

**Results.** Conversion rises from **8.4% to 19.1%**, worth **+$31M** in first‑year
revenue and **+0.4 products** per relationship. The result then splits the cohort
into segments, each with its own best offer: scaling multi‑site businesses take a
pre‑approved line at **26%** versus **9%** today; off‑us financers take the card
win‑back at **22%** versus **7%**. And about **2,900** businesses are placed in a
watch group with no offer — their modeled return doesn't clear the cost to serve.

## 4 · SMB deposit defense — "Minimum‑effective‑rate"  *(small business)*

**Hypothesis.** **41,200** business operating accounts — **$3.4B** in deposits — are
showing early outflow and rate‑shopping, with no signs of business distress: these
are healthy balances being courted on rate. At today's blended rate of **1.10%**,
about **$54M** of net interest income is exposed. The hypothesis: retain each
account with the smallest rate increase that actually holds it, rather than
matching the competitor across the board. Pre‑simulation estimate: **$34–46M**
retained, at a blended **+32 to +44 basis points** versus **+95** to match.

**Configuration.** I scope to the full at‑risk book. I set the minimum account
balance to **$25K**, the rate uplift to **+38 basis points** — the modeled
minimum‑effective level — and a hard rate ceiling of **3.75%** so no single offer
can exceed it. I deliver through a banker for large balances and in‑app for the
rest, with a **10% holdout**. The margin floor and the no‑overpay rule are enforced.

**Results.** The policy retains **$2.6B** of the **$3.4B** at risk and **$41M** of the
**$54M** in exposed income, at a blended **+38 basis points**. Matching the
competitor would have cost **+95** — so this retains comparable balances for about
**$13M a year less**. It prices by segment: **+55 bps** for the high‑balance
rate‑driven accounts, **+35** for mid‑balance, **+25** for the relationship‑anchored.
It makes no offer to roughly **7,400** accounts modeled to stay regardless, and
declines the accounts that would fall below the margin floor.

---

### The thread through all four
Each follows the same path: a hypothesis grounded in real signals → a policy set
on a few clear levers → a simulation that returns the outcome and the cost — and,
every time, an explicit decision on where **not** to act. That restraint is what
makes the recommended actions credible.
