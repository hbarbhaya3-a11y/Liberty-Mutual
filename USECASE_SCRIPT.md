# Narration Script — say it out loud

**How to use:** read the **bold** lines aloud. The *(italic)* lines are what you
click/do. Short sentences on purpose — easy to say without tripping. ~40 sec each.

---

## Open — on the Cockpit
*(stay on the cockpit, don't click)*
> **Everything starts here. Each tile is a pattern the system spotted in the bank's own data — money moving, customers shifting, balances under pressure.**
> **The ones I'll walk through are right at the top.**

---

## 1 · Deposit drift  *(consumer)*
*(click "Mass affluent · deposit drift")*
> **This one's about savings quietly walking out the door. A group of our better‑off savers are starting to move their money to banks that pay more.**
> **We can't just give everyone a raise — that's expensive. So the bet is simple: find the ones actually about to leave, and pay only them just enough to keep them.**

*(click "Test this hypothesis" → pick If‑What)*
> **I don't build the offer myself. I tell it the goal — keep the most money for the least cost — and let it find the plan.**

*(click Run → results land)*
> **It keeps about nineteen million dollars in deposits. And here's the discipline — it refuses to pay the people who'd have stayed anyway. That money would've been thrown away.**

---

## 2 · Idle cash
*(click "Idle‑cash liquidity activation")*
> **A simple one. Three hundred and eighty‑eight thousand customers have cash just sitting there earning almost nothing — while competitors advertise four percent. Some have already started moving it.**
> **So we offer those people a seven‑month CD that pays more — but only the money they don't need day to day.**

*(Test → If‑What)*
> **Again — I just set the goal, earn the most interest income, and which products it's allowed to use. It does the rest.**

*(Run → results)*
> **Four‑point‑three million dollars in extra relationship value, and about two hundred sixty‑five million in balances moved into yield — and the money leaving drops from twelve percent to six. The clever part — it leaves everyday spending cash alone, so nobody's locked out of money they need.**

---

## 3 · SMB growth  *(small business)*
*(click "SMBs entering an expansion cycle")*
> **Now small business. These are companies that are growing — and most of them are already getting their loans and card processing from a competitor, even though they bank with us.**
> **So we go win it back. Lead with the one product each business needs right now, then bring its everyday banking along with it.**

*(Test → If‑What)*
> **I tell it the goal — get the most businesses to say yes — and which offers it can make: a credit line, a card, equipment financing, a bundle.**

*(Run → results)*
> **Acceptance jumps from eight percent to nineteen — about thirty‑one million in new revenue. And notice: it gives each type of business a different offer, and it flags the ones that aren't worth chasing. It tells us where not to spend.**

---

## 4 · SMB deposit defense  *(small business)*
*(click "Rate‑sensitive SMB operating balances")*
> **Last one — same money‑walking‑out problem, but for businesses. These are healthy companies; they're just shopping for a better rate.**
> **So we give each one just enough of a bump to keep it — and not a penny more.**

*(Test → If‑What)*
> **Goal — keep the most money for the least cost. And I set a hard ceiling, so it can never overpay.**

*(Run → results)*
> **It keeps two‑point‑six billion dollars for a small rate bump — versus more than double that to fully match the competitor. That's about thirteen million dollars saved. And it walks away from the ones that would've stayed, and the ones that cost too much to keep.**

---

## Close
*(back on the Cockpit)*
> **That's the pattern every time. Spot it, make the bet, let the tool find the best plan inside the rules, see the result, send it to deploy.**
> **And every single time, it tells us where not to act — which is exactly what makes the moves it does recommend believable.**
